Writes per second of konsensd when the checks of a step run on a pool of threads

What was measured
- Arms: base dddb05e (no pool) and parallel-check-run 9b55df7 (pool), each in g++-16, g++-15 and clang++-22 Release, each its own binary. A second build of the g++-16 pool (g16poolB) for A/A. The pool binary runs with check_threads = 0, 1 and 2 in the TOML file of each node.
- bench/arms/check-threads.sh (sha256 734f6a5fdbc925dd) with bench/arms/cluster3.sh (sha256 8d83a5fe926f111a): the client goes to the leader after no node changed its state for 3 s. 64 in flight, 3 samples of 8192 writes of 64 bytes per start, the median of the 3 samples per round, 4 rounds, the arms in turn inside each round. sync_by_the_caller = true, Unix sockets.
- podman --cpus=3, user 1001, not pinned, seccomp profile with io_uring, kernel 6.18.44-fc-v185, 4 vCPU Intel Xeon, three btrfs loop images compress=zstd:1 on one host disk. The three nodes and their pool threads share the 3 cpus. The run ended 2026-10-31 20:21 UTC by the clock of the machine.
- cpu = user + sys us per write, summed over the three nodes and their threads.

Noise
- A/A: g16pool:0 13532 against g16poolB:0 13812 writes/s, 2.1 %. The rounds of one arm spread up to 11 % (g16poolB 13322 to 14777).

Result
- With 1 or 2 threads, konsensd is slower in all three builds, by 11 to 19 %, and uses 30 to 44 us more cpu per write. The latency grows by 600 to 1000 us.
- With 0 threads, g15 and c22 lose 4 to 5 %, above the A/A of 2.1 %; g16 shows no difference.
- Not measured: which part of the pool costs the time. Each range replays the results from the committed applied index to its first entry, and each job is one MSG_RING round trip (24 to 31 us, 2026-10-31-1935Z) and one read transaction. On this machine the pool threads of the three nodes share 3 cpus. The result on three machines is not known.
