Writes per second of konsensd when a result is read as views into the bytes of the log

What was measured
- Arms: base dddb05e and result-views 82cdd38, each in g++-16, g++-15 and clang++-22 Release, and a second build of the g++-16 base (g16baseB) for A/A. Each arm is its own binary.
- bench/arms/cap-of-the-step.sh (sha256 fd88d1cc864ca404) with bench/arms/cluster3.sh (sha256 8d83a5fe926f111a): the client goes to the leader after no node changed its state for 3 s. 64 in flight, 3 samples of 8192 writes of 64 bytes per start, the median of the 3 samples per round, 6 rounds, the arms in turn inside each round. sync_by_the_caller = true, Unix sockets.
- podman --cpus=3, user 1001, not pinned, seccomp profile with io_uring, kernel 6.18.44-fc-v185, 4 vCPU Intel Xeon, three btrfs loop images compress=zstd:1 on one host disk. The run ended 2026-10-31 19:43 UTC by the clock of the machine. That clock is behind the time stamps of the earlier result files of this day.
- cpu = user + sys us per write, summed over the three nodes.

Noise
- A/A: g16base 13391 against g16baseB 13484 writes/s, 0.7 %; cpu 100 against 102 us/write. The rounds of one arm spread up to 25 % (g16baseB 11965 to 14923), most arms 10 to 14 %.

Result
- Every arm of result-views has a median above its base, by 0.9 to 2.9 %, and a cpu per write at most equal to its base. Each difference is inside the spread of the rounds of both arms. So this run shows no difference in writes/s that stands above the noise.
- The rate follows the latency of a write (2026-10-31-1920Z). A smaller cpu cost on the step does not change that latency at 64 in flight.
